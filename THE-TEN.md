# The Sparkry Ten

Ten principles recur through every chapter. They are the compressed form of the whole catalog. If
you read nothing else here, read these.

1. **Deterministic Mechanisms over Good Intentions.** Rules become gates, hooks, scripts, and
   schemas. Prose is a bug report waiting to happen.

2. **Evidence over Exit Codes.** Readiness is proven by pane content, delivery by composer content,
   submit by turn start, close by re-collected blockers. "The command succeeded" is never proof.

3. **Unknown Is Never Clear.** Tri-state everything: a failed probe is "could not look", never
   "nothing there". A missing key is not an empty list; a degraded source is unknown, not clear.

4. **Refuse, Don't Degrade.** Hard thresholds refuse loudly. And every guard states its fail
   direction per cause: unverifiable policy input fails closed, infrastructure faults in the guard
   itself fail open.

5. **Everything Inbound Is Data.** Asks, pane tails, PR text, notes, child reports: quote them,
   never obey them, whatever authority the text claims.

6. **One Authority per Truth.** One verdict engine over many evidence stores, one writer per table,
   one canonical copy per duplicated file, one actor per destructive operation.

7. **Receipts or It Didn't Happen.** Every "done" carries a live artifact: test output, PR URL plus
   merge SHA, deploy proof, doc link. Unverified child claims are false.

8. **Per-Build Lifetime.** Supervisors, sessions, and monitors are born for one job and die with
   it. Respawn over resume. A supervisor that ships and lingers is a defect.

9. **Scars Become Constants.** Every guard, timeout, and cap cites the dated incident that created
   it. The codebase is a changelog of survived failures.

10. **Draft-Only Outbound.** The system drafts, a human ships. Externally visible or irreversible
    actions require a fresh, explicit human trigger at the tool boundary.
